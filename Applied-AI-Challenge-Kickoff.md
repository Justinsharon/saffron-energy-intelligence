## 10. Worked Case: The Intelligent Admissions CRM
Presented in the session "Applied AI: Case Walkthrough" by **Divyashish Jindal, AD, Masters' Union** `[Slides 20–21]`. A worked example of how to break a problem down layer by layer.

### Layer 1 · Context `[Slide 22]`
A new programme, launching from zero: **small counselling team · limited budget · zero historical conversion data.**

### Layer 2 · How it works today `[Slide 23]`
First come, first served: **New lead** (any source) → **Lead Matrix** (internal CRM) → **Next counsellor** (auto-assigned, in order).

What counsellors said:
- Many leads are unreachable or irrelevant.
- High-intent leads wait in the same queue.

*Discussion prompt `[Slide 24]`: Too many leads? Weak counsellors? Something else?*

### Layer 3 · The real problem `[Slide 25]`
**How might we use applied AI to decide…**
1. which leads deserve a counsellor's time,
2. which counsellor should take them,
3. and what to say,

…so a capacity-constrained admissions team converts more applicants, **without growing headcount**.

### Layer 4 · The binding constraint `[Slide 26]`
What is the scarce resource?
- **200** calls a day, at most, per counsellor.
- **2.5–3 hours** of actual talk time a day.
- **The order of the call list decides the outcome.**

### Layer 5 · What made it hard `[Slide 27]`
Six things working against them:
1. **Thin data at entry** — only name, phone, email, education stage, work experience, source and medium.
2. **Cold start** — no past outcomes to train a model on.
3. **Uneven strengths** — some counsellors convert fresh graduates better, others working professionals.
4. **Unused call data** — every call recorded, none analysed.
5. **Low-intent volume** — some sources flood the queue. No pre-sales team, and no AI voice calling, by choice.
6. **Manual status quo** — other teams export to Excel, sort by hand and allocate on gut feel.

### Layer 6 · Break it down: four sub-problems `[Slide 28]`
| # | Sub-problem | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Prioritise | Rank leads so counsellors call the most promising first. |
| 2 | Match | Route each lead to the counsellor most likely to convert it. |
| 3 | Learn | Turn call recordings into objections, counsellor quality and new lead facts. |
| 4 | Pre-qualify | Filter low-intent leads so only engaged ones reach a counsellor. |

*Discussion prompt `[Slide 29]`: Which would you solve first, and how? Constraints: no new hires, no historical data, no AI voice calls.*

### The answer: a closed loop on the existing CRM `[Slide 30]`
**Score** every lead → **Route** to the right counsellor → **Learn** from every call → **Feed back** (re-score, coach, nurture).
Held to: no new headcount · no AI voice calling · shipped piece by piece.

### How it was built `[Slide 31]`
One MU student, inside admissions — an applied AI builder who owned the problem end to end, with the programme lead.
1. Sat in on calls → 2. Interviewed counsellors → 3. Prototyped each module → 4. Shipped into the dashboard.
*Every stage started from a complaint on the floor, not from a technology.*

### The solution: five stages at a glance `[Slide 32]`
| Stage | What broke | The AI answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1 · Score and route | Calling unreachable leads | Lead score + counsellor matching |
| 2 · Call intelligence | Calls recorded, never used | Transcription + LLM extraction |
| 3 · Progressive profile | Too little known per lead | Call facts written back, live re-scoring |
| 4 · Conversion assist | Same objections, uneven answers | Ideal-answer prompts + objection nudges |
| 5 · Pre-qualification | Low-intent leads clog the queue | Drip with CTAs; engaged leads get a call |

### Architecture: one closed loop `[Slide 33]`
`Input: New lead` → `Stage 1: Score & route` → `Human counsellor call` → `Stage 2: Call intelligence` → (every call) → `Stage 3: Live profile` (feeds back into Stage 1).
- Low-intent leads → `Stage 5: Drip & CTAs`; once engaged → into the counsellor queue.
- `Stage 4: Ideal answers` supports the counsellor call.
- Stage 2 also outputs **Objections → To marketing**.

### Stage 1 · Score and route
**A score that works on day one** `[Slide 34]` — six signals, weighted by team judgement, summed to 0–100.

| Signal | Weighted up (illustrative) |
|---|---|
| Source | High-intent first: search ads, webinars, re-engaged applicants, referrals |
| Medium / campaign | Campaigns with better connect and app-start rates |
| Work experience | Working professionals: early paid applications skewed here |
| Education stage | Graduates eligible now, above those not yet eligible |
| Data quality | Valid phone and email; duplicates and junk penalised |
| Recency | Fresh leads boosted; the score decays with age |

**Route it to the right counsellor** `[Slide 35]`
- **Match:** each lead goes to the counsellor best at that segment, with free capacity. A daily cap stops top counsellors being flooded.
- **Dashboard:** a ranked queue — the score, the reason for it, and the lead's history in one view.
- **Upgrade path:** once outcomes exist, calibrate or replace the weights with logistic regression or gradient-boosted trees.
- *Start with rules you can explain. Move to models when data earns it.*

### Stage 2 · Call intelligence
**Turn recordings into intelligence** `[Slide 36]` — every call was already recorded; none of it was used.
1. **Transcribe** — speech-to-text, speakers separated, Hindi-English handled.
2. **Extract** — an LLM reads each transcript against a fixed schema.
3. **Classify** — objections to a taxonomy; answers rated on a rubric.
4. **Store** — written back to the CRM, against lead and counsellor.

Objection taxonomy: Fees · ROI · Placements · Time commitment · Credibility · Curriculum depth · Parental approval.

**Three outputs, three owners** `[Slide 37]`
| Output | Used by | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Objections by segment and source | Marketing | Answer them in ads, webinars, FAQs and site copy, before the call |
| Answer quality by counsellor | Admissions lead | Targeted coaching; best answers become the playbook |
| Facts about the lead | CRM (Stage 3) | Enrich the profile and update the score |

Safeguards:
- **Fixed taxonomy** — you can count it and trend it; free-text summaries you can't.
- **Weekly spot-check** — a human reviews a sample of extractions to catch drift.
- **Consent first** — recording and analysis follow consent and data-protection rules.

### Stage 3 · Progressive profile — a score that keeps learning `[Slide 38]`
| Explicit (stated on the call) | Implicit (inferred from behaviour) |
|---|---|
| Role and company | Objections raised |
| Years of experience | Sentiment |
| Target role | Questions asked |
| Preferred start | Email and WhatsApp clicks |
| Financing need | Webinar attendance |
| Self or parents decide | Application progress |

The score moves after every interaction. **Up:** clear target role, a financing plan. **Down:** hard fee objection, no follow-up. Then the queue re-ranks itself.

### Stage 4 · Conversion assist
**Everyone answers like the best** `[Slide 39]`
- Best real answers: top-rated responses from Stage 2.
- Distilled to 3–4 key points per objection.
- Shown on the dashboard before and during the call, triggered when a lead's profile shows that objection.

**Answer it before the next call** `[Slide 40]` — a logged objection triggers a short email and WhatsApp sequence.
| Objection | Pre-call nudge |
|---|---|
| Fees / ROI | Fee structure, scholarships and financing options |
| Placements / outcomes | Faculty and industry mentor profiles, curriculum projects |
| Time commitment | The weekly schedule and format, explained |
| Credibility | Faculty background, industry partners, campus |
| Parental approval | A parent-facing explainer to share at home |

*The next call starts further down the funnel.*

### Stage 5 · Pre-qualification — low-intent leads earn their call `[Slide 41]`
1. Low scores from flagged sources go to a nurture track, not the queue.
2. Each email offers a CTA: an AI career-path diagnostic, a webinar, a live session, a masterclass.
3. Every action adds points to the score and data to the profile.
4. Past a threshold, the lead joins the counsellor queue, with context.
5. No engagement: low-cost nurture and retargeting.

**A deliberate choice — why not AI voice calling?** For a premium programme, the first human conversation is part of the product. A poor automated call damages the brand.

### Measuring impact: judged on the funnel `[Slide 42]`
Before versus after each stage went live.
| Metric | What it tells you | Stages |
|---|---|---|
| Connect rate per counsellor-hour | Are we calling reachable, relevant leads? | 1, 5 |
| Lead → application started | Is prioritisation finding intent? | 1, 3, 5 |
| Application started → fee paid | Are objections being resolved? | 2, 4 |
| Conversion by counsellor and segment | Are routing and coaching working? | 1, 2, 4 |
| Talk time on high-score leads | Is capacity going where it should? | 1, 5 |
| Top objections over time | Is marketing pre-empting them? | 2 |

### Lessons: take these into your challenge `[Slide 43]`
1. **Start from the bottleneck** — here, counsellor talk time. Every module spends it better.
2. **Rules first, models later** — a transparent score on day one; ML once outcomes exist.
3. **Unstructured data is the hidden asset** — the recordings existed; the value was in structuring them.
4. **Close the loop** — call insights feed scoring, routing, coaching, marketing and nurture.
5. **Know when not to automate** — no AI voice calling; the human conversation was protected on purpose.
6. **Build inside the existing stack** — extending the CRM beat replacing it; adoption came free.

## 11. Your Challenge: Five-Step Method `[Slide 45]`
| Step | Guidance | Case example |
|---|---|---|
| **Frame** | The business problem, not the AI one. One line: user, outcome, constraint. | Convert more applicants with a fixed team |
| **Find** | The single scarce resource that caps the outcome. | Counsellor talk time |
| **Map** | What data exists, what builds up over time, what is missing. | Thin lead fields, unused call recordings |
| **Build** | The simplest thing that works, then layer. Decide what not to automate. | Rules first, ML later, no AI voice calls |
| **Prove** | Name the baseline, the target and how you will measure it. **No metric: a demo, not a solution.** | — |

## 12. Running the Client Call

### A 20-minute call `[Slide 46]`
| Time | Focus | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| 0–3 min | Confirm understanding | "Here is how we read the problem. What did we get wrong?" |
| 3–8 min | Metric and ROI | "Which number must move, by how much, by when? What is a win worth?" |
| 8–13 min | Constraints | "What data can we access? Team, budget, tools? Anything off-limits?" |
| 13–17 min | History | "What have you tried? Why did it not work? Who does this by hand?" |
| 17–20 min | Users and close | "Who uses this daily? What would make them adopt or reject it?" |

### Rules and traps `[Slide 47]`
| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Rank 8–10 questions. You will not get through more. | **Solution first.** "Let's build an agent" before you know the bottleneck. |
| Split roles: one asks, one takes notes, one watches the clock. | **Imaginary data.** The cold-start problem catches most teams. |
| Ask "why" twice on the answer that matters most. | **Over-automation.** Justify where you kept a human. |
| Don't pitch. You are there to learn. | **No feedback loop.** Each use should improve the next decision. |
